What the Šikuta Method is

Slow breathing that gradually deepens. Breathe in through your nose like a wave, pause briefly, breathe out longer through your mouth. Each round you breathe a little slower and deeper, and the session ends with a calm breath hold.

The basic rhythm is 4 · 2 · 6: in for 4 seconds, hold on full lungs for 2 seconds, out for 6 seconds. The out-breath is always longer than the in-breath. Slow breathing with a longer exhale is how the body calms itself, and the method is built on that.

What sets it apart from the other Breathune programs is that the rhythm does not stand still. Box breathing or coherent 5-5 breathing keep the same pace from start to finish. The Šikuta Method starts at a comfortable pace and slows it down gradually, so you reach calm step by step without forcing long breaths right at the start.

Step by step

1

A calm start

Sit or lie down. Breathe quietly for a few seconds, then take a first, deeper breath.

2

In through your nose, like a wave (4 seconds)

First the belly gently rises, then the chest. Smoothly, without forcing it. Put a hand on your belly and feel it lift.

3

A short hold (2 seconds)

Stay on full lungs for a moment. Keep your throat and shoulders relaxed.

4

Out through your mouth (6 seconds)

Let everything go. The out-breath is longer and slower than the in-breath and goes all the way out. That is where the calm comes in.

5

A deepening wave

By default there are three rounds of two and a half minutes with a short pause. With each round the in-breath gets about one and a half seconds longer and the out-breath about two seconds longer. Don't rush. Let the wave carry you.

6

A calm finish

At the end you hold your breath on full lungs and then on empty lungs, for as long as it feels comfortable. Then breathe in slowly.

How often? Once a day is enough, for example in the evening before sleep or whenever you need to pause during the day. The whole session takes about 9 minutes, and you can change the number and length of rounds in the app.

What you may feel and what to watch out for

A gentle ringing in the ears, a shift in body warmth or tingling in the fingers are common during longer holds. Just notice them and carry on. You can always take a breath sooner, and that's perfectly fine. The final hold is not a contest.

Do not practise while driving, in water, or standing up if you tend to feel dizzy. If you are pregnant or have a heart or lung condition, talk to a doctor before practising breath holds. If you feel unwell, stop.

Frequently asked questions

How long does the Šikuta Method take?

By default three rounds of two and a half minutes with a short pause between them and a calm finish, about 9 minutes in total. You can change the number and length of rounds in the app.

Do I have to hold my breath for a whole minute?

No. The final hold is not a contest. You can take a breath sooner at any time and the app carries on. If you like, press and hold the centre of the screen to extend the hold.

Is the Šikuta Method safe?

For a healthy adult it is calm, slow breathing. Do not practise it while driving, in water, or standing up if you tend to feel dizzy. If you are pregnant or have a heart or lung condition, talk to a doctor before practising breath holds.

Is the Šikuta Method free in Breathune?
