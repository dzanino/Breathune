Why a breathing exercise helps you fall asleep

Falling asleep is not about "switching your head off" — it is a physiological shift from an alert, sympathetic state to a calm, parasympathetic one. Breath is the one part of that shift you can directly control.

When you're lying in bed with your mind circling tomorrow's tasks, the body is still in a mild state of alert — shallow, fast breathing only sustains it. A prolonged exhale and a slow, regular breathing pace activate the vagus nerve and trigger the parasympathetic response: heart rate slows, muscles relax, the body receives the signal that it is safe to sleep. Unlike counting sheep, a breathing exercise gives the mind a concrete, simple task — following a rhythm — instead of letting it wander among thoughts.

Three techniques that work before bed

4-7-8 breathing

Inhale for 4, hold for 7, exhale for 8 counts. An extremely long exhale makes this the fastest route to calm — ideal right before lights out.

Coherent 5-5 breathing

An even 5-second inhale and exhale, with no holds at all — suited to anyone who finds breath-holding uncomfortable. Evens out heart-rate variability.

Frequently asked questions

Which breathing exercise is best for falling asleep?

4-7-8 is the most commonly recommended, for its long, markedly calming exhale, or Coherent 5-5 breathing with no holds for anyone who finds breath-holding uncomfortable. The Breathune app guides both right in bed, quietly and without a bright display.

How long before bed should I practise a breathing exercise?

3 to 5 minutes right before lights out, lying in bed with your eyes closed, is enough. Consistency matters more than duration — the same ritual every evening signals to the body that it is time to sleep.

Does a breathing exercise also help with snoring?

Indirectly, yes — better, calmer sleep reduces the number of awakenings that accompany snoring. To address snoring directly, Breathune has a separate Snore No More module with throat-muscle training and overnight tracking.
