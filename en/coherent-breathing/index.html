Five seconds in, five seconds out, no holds. At this pace your breathing and heart rate fall into step. What happens in your body, what the studies say, and how to start.

Normal and slow pace

At rest, an adult breathes roughly 9 to 24 times a minute. Coherent breathing slows you down to six breaths a minute.

Breath and heart

Your heart rate rises slightly as you breathe in and falls as you breathe out. This respiratory sinus arrhythmia is normal in a healthy person and is controlled by the vagus nerve.

At six breaths a minute, these swings in heart rate grow larger and turn into a regular wave. Lehrer and Gevirtz explain this as resonance with the baroreflex, which uses pressure receptors in the aorta and carotid artery to steady blood pressure. At this pace, breathing and the reflex reinforce each other.

The ideal pace differs a little from person to person. In their measurements the average was around 5.5 breaths a minute, and taller people and men resonated slightly more slowly. A 5-5 rhythm is a good starting point, not a rule.

What the studies found

Laborde and colleagues (2022) analysed 223 studies of slow breathing. Measures of heart rate variability that reflect vagus nerve activity rose during the exercise, right after it, and after several weeks of practice. The authors see slow breathing as a low-cost aid for prevention and an add-on to treatment, with few adverse effects expected.

Higher heart rate variability is not a treatment, though. Coherent breathing does not replace treatment for high blood pressure, anxiety or depression, and how much it helps varies from person to person.

How to start

  • Sit upright or lie down and let your shoulders drop.
  • Breathe in through your nose for five seconds, smoothly and without effort.
  • Breathe out for five seconds without stopping.
  • Start with five minutes and add more later.
  • If you feel dizzy or your fingers tingle, you are breathing too deeply. Breathe more shallowly and keep the pace. If it doesn't pass, stop.

If five seconds feels too long, start with four and lengthen gradually.
