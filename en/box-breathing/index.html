What box breathing is, and why it works

Four equally long phases — inhale, hold, exhale, hold — forming a square you can picture, or even draw. In Breathune this program is called Focus; "box breathing" is its internationally established name. Its simplicity is the point: nothing to remember, only a rhythm to follow.

Box breathing was popularised by US military units as a fast way to stay composed under pressure, but the physiology behind it is the same as in other slow-breathing techniques for anxiety. A slow, even pace (one cycle lasts 16 seconds, under four breaths per minute) combined with brief holds activates the parasympathetic nervous system via the vagus nerve — the body receives the signal that the danger has passed, heart rate slows and muscles relax.

Unlike the Šikuta Method in Breathune, which deepens cycle by cycle, box breathing keeps a constant, predictable rhythm from start to finish. That makes it a natural choice right before a stressful moment — an interview, an exam, a difficult conversation — when you need to lower tension quickly and reliably without tracking which phase of the exercise you are in.

Step by step

1

Inhale through the nose — 4 seconds

Breathe in slowly and smoothly through the nose. The belly should rise a moment before the chest.

2

Hold on full lungs — 4 seconds

Hold your breath without tensing your throat or shoulders. If it feels uncomfortable, shorten it to 2–3 seconds.

3

Exhale through the mouth — 4 seconds

Release the air slowly, as if blowing through a straw. The exhale should match the inhale in length.

4

Hold on empty lungs — 4 seconds

Stay without air for a moment, then inhale smoothly again and repeat the cycle.

How many cycles? Four to six cycles (about 1–2 minutes) are enough for a noticeable calming effect. For a deeper effect, practise 5 minutes a day, ideally at a consistent time — in the morning before a demanding day, or in the evening before sleep.

Frequently asked questions

How long should one box-breathing cycle last?

The classic ratio is 4-4-4-4 seconds (inhale–hold–exhale–hold), or 16 seconds per cycle. Beginners can start with 3-3-3-3; the Breathune app keeps the exact rhythm with visuals, sound and haptics, so you never have to count yourself.

Is box breathing safe for everyone?

For a healthy adult, yes — it is a gentle technique with no strain involved. If you are pregnant or have respiratory or cardiovascular conditions, check with a physician before practising regular breath-holds. You can always inhale sooner than the rhythm calls for.
